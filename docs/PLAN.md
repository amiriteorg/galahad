# The feature plan

What Galahad builds next, in order, and why. Each item says which of the [mandate's](MANDATE.md) numbers it should move (correctness, horizon, cost, reversibility; the north star is human interventions per completed task) and how that will be told. Ideas come from the owner, from other agents (Hermes Agent, Grok Bot, OpenAI Dots, OpenMausBot, Rakazo) and from proposals. Some were declined: the end says which, and why.

✅ built · 🔨 being built · 🔜 planned · ✋ declined

## Next, in order

What breaks today comes first, then what costs too much or proves too little, then what Galahad can't do yet. A number names an item, not its place: one added later goes where it belongs and keeps its number.

| # | What | Moves, and how it's told | From | State |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Galahad leads in rooms.** What you say to a room without naming a knight is his: he answers, and brings in only the knights the work needs. | Cost: one turn of his and theirs, not one of every knight's. Interventions: you ask the room, not each knight. | Grok Bot's chief of staff; Rakazo's and OpenMausBot's room routing | ✅ 16 Nov 2026 |
| 2 | **Knights' computers that grow.** A knight's VM gets more cores, memory or disk while it runs, with no restart and its work kept (a real microVM went from 2 to 4 cores, 4 to 6 GB and 1 to 3 GB of disk live): yours to give (`galahad roundtable resize`, **Give it more**) ✅. A knight's own VM takes no slot, only its memory of the ceiling, and its threads while it works, so resting knights no longer leave other work waiting ✅ (on the owner's server five resting VMs had held 14 of its 16 threads, and no quest could start). Galahad gives a VM more himself when its work needs it, within the ceiling, the ledger saying it was him and why (✅ 16 Nov, your ruling). Next: a knight's disk grown by itself when it runs low (not needed yet: the server's knights' disks were 1 to 6 % full). Shrinking happens when the VM is made again. | Horizon: no task dies of a full disk or of memory. Told by tasks that fail with no space or out of memory, and by sandboxes that wait for a slot. | The owner | 🔨 |
| 3 | **Cheaper coding plans.** Xiaomi MiMo's Token Plan, MiniMax's M Plan, Alibaba's Token Plan, and Volcengine's, BytePlus's, StepFun's, Tencent's and Baidu's plans join OpenCode Go, Z.ai, Kimi and Alibaba's Coding Plan as presets (✅ 16 Nov), each used only as far as its vendor's own terms allow; NanoGPT, Synthetic and Ollama's cloud are flat-rate providers whose terms set no such rule. | Cost per completed task, from the census. | The owner | ✅ |
| 4 | **Chores on cheap models.** Every background call names its role: folding a long chat and a room's story are on the swift role (✅ 16 Nov; a room's falls back to the knight's own role when swift has no model that answers), as understanding the archive and the heartbeat's checklist already were; distilling a skill is on the cheapest role that answers; looking at a screen is the eyes role's. A chore runs on a role named after it once you hire a model for that (`galahad roster set fold <provider/model>`: folding chats and rooms), on swift until then ✅. The archive stays on swift's local models unless you give it a budget. | Cost: tokens a day by chore, in the census. | Hermes' auxiliary models | ✅ 16 Nov 2026 |
| 5 | **Lean context.** A long chat is folded into a story past a threshold, as rooms are already (✅ 16 Nov). In a quest, a command's output that a later run of the same command superseded becomes a line once the conversation is cut short, never in the middle of the cached beginning (✅ 16 Nov: a quest that runs its tests after each of 13 edits was sent 11 % less in all, its last turn 21 % less). So is a file's contents a later command changed (✅ 16 Nov: a quest that reads, edits and tests ten files was sent 10 % less in all, its last turn 14 % less). Next: an error since fixed; and skills given by their names and what each is for, each read whole only when the work needs it. Exact diffs, paths and the latest error stay whole. | Cost and correctness: tokens per task and pass rate in the trials. | Hermes' compression; a proposal for semantic garbage collection; Anthropic's Agent Skills (progressive disclosure) | 🔨 |
| 6 | **Switch, and escalate when stuck.** A command that fails exactly as it did before is said to (after an edit: what changed didn't change it), and the same failure three times in a row ends the quest rather than spending more (✅ 16 Nov, quests and knights' tasks). When the same gate fails twice in a row, the rest of the work goes to the planner's models, the reviewer staying independent of it (✅ 16 Nov: his quests and the knights' tasks; the `escalate` trial variant measures it). Next: Galahad moves the rest of a turn to another role, down for repetitive work. | Cost and correctness: dollars and pass rate per task in the trials. | Hermes' `model_switch`; a proposal for loop circuit breakers | 🔨 |
| 7 | **Contract first.** A quest's gates (yours, and the planner's own) already run before any work and after. The test files a gate names are locked: a submission that changed one goes back before any gate runs on it (✅ 16 Nov). Next: a gate that passes before any work is refused as proof, since it proves nothing; and the engineer may work on a cheaper role while the strong one writes the contract. | Correctness and cost: pass rate, reverts and dollars per task in the trials. | A proposal for contract gates; this repository's own rule, failing test first | 🔨 |
| 8 | **A budget per task, in dollars.** A task is given an envelope and sees what's left at each step. Near its end it wraps up or asks; at the end it stops with what it has. A knight's task given a budget (`--budget-usd`) is told what it has spent at each step, wraps up past four fifths, and stops with what it has, which the gates and the reviewer still judge (✅ 16 Nov, in the CLI, the app's two give-a-task sheets and the iPhone's), and so is `galahad do --budget-usd`, one budget for all its attempts (✅ 16 Nov). A coding tool's own calls through the gateway count in a knight's task's budget too (✅ 16 Nov, a VM on the node running the task), and in `galahad do`'s (✅ 16 Nov, its sandboxes asked of their nodes at each step); a coding tool is stopped once its budget is spent, and what it left is judged (✅ 16 Nov: before, only the engineer's own loop was held to a budget). Next: a knight's VM on another node. | Cost: no runaway task. Told by the census: spend per task against its envelope. | A proposal for compute economics | 🔨 |
| 23 | **Hooks and tripwires, yours.** Rules you write that run at fixed points of the work: before a command or a tool call (halt it, or ask you: a tripwire such as "a command that touches `*.lock` halts"), after one (run a check, tell you), before an answer goes out. Deterministic and sandboxed: never a model's judgment and never code an agent wrote, versioned and signed like a standing order. Standing orders already answer outward actions in advance; hooks reach what runs inside the work. Tripwires on commands ✅ 16 Nov: a rule of yours is globs matched against a command's words as the tokenizer splits them, and if you like a piece of text it must hold; it halts the work, or refuses that command and he is told which rule and why. Checked before every command of his quests and of a knight's task on his own runner, and every gate; each trip a line in the ledger (`galahad rule`, Tripwires on the Loops page). A coding tool's own commands inside its VM aren't seen: only its gates are. Checks after a command ✅ 16 Nov: the same globs and text, and a command of yours that runs where his work runs once a command it matches has succeeded; he is told at once if it fails (not one of his commands, never after a gate; the reviewer sees it in the trail). Next: a rule that tells you when a command it matches has run, and rules before an answer goes out. | Interventions and cost: questions you no longer answer, model calls a tripwire stops before they are paid for. Told by approvals a day and halted runs in the census. | Claude Code's hooks; the OpenAI Agents SDK's guardrails and tripwires; a gap analysis you sent (16 Nov) | 🔨 |
| 15 | **Checkpoint, rewind and fork.** A quest keeps a checkpoint at each step (its conversation and its workspace as they stood), so it can be paused and taken up on another node, rewound to a step, or forked there: when the planner names two approaches, both are worked from the same checkpoint and the gates and the independent reviewer pick one. (`--effort` already forks, but at the start; the mind's log already replays to any state, and work cut off by a planned restart is taken up again, but a quest's own steps can't be rewound.) | Reversibility, horizon and correctness, its cost bounded by the effort budget: time from a bad step back to clean ground; pass rate and dollars per task in the trials. | A proposal for speculative forks; LangGraph's checkpoints and time travel; Codex's saved rollouts | 🔜 |
| 9 | **Trials from your own work.** Each task that passed review (its goal, its repository at the base commit, its gates) is kept as a trial case. A change to a harness, a role or a routing rule re-runs the hardest of them in sandboxes, before and after. | Every number above: the scorecard on your own work, not only on public sets ([TRIALS](TRIALS.md) plans held-out sets). | The mandate; a proposal for shadow replay | 🔜 |
| 10 | **A decider on your own machine.** The `decider` role answers typed decisions (yes or no, one of several, a score) with probabilities. TypeSafe's Jev fills it (on a real node since 16 November 2026, through your Cloudflare AI Gateway). A small model on the node (llama.cpp) is to answer the same way, by reading its next-token probabilities over the options it was given and normalising across those alone, with no text generated. That is SemIf's method (formerly OpenJev), run on the node instead of in a browser. | Cost: decisions and chores (#4) at no API cost, and nothing leaves the machine. Told by a decision set in the trials: accuracy and calibration (Brier score) against Jev's, before it decides anything that matters. | SemIf (openjev.com) | 🔜 |
| 11 | **Galahad's own computer.** A persistent VM with a desktop that is his, kept between conversations. You watch it and take it over as you do a knight's, and he works on it with a `computer` tool. | Horizon: work that needs a browser or a GUI, done by him. | Grok Bot's cloud computer; Dots' containers | ✅ 16 Nov 2026 |
| 12 | **Knights' verified work teaches skills.** A knight's task that passes review teaches a skill as his own quests do (it waits for your approval: a knight's VM is online), and a knight's task is given his skills that bear on its goal, its planner and engineer, never its reviewer (✅ 16 Nov). Next: told by the second similar task's interventions, once there are enough of them. | Horizon: interventions on the second similar task. | Hermes' curator | ✅ |
| 13 | **Event triggers.** A loop can wake on a signed webhook, new mail on a connector, a GitHub event (a failed CI run, a pushed branch), not only on a schedule, with a cooldown so a burst wakes it once. | Horizon: work started without you asking. | Dots' triggers; a proposal for ambient triggers | 🔜 |
| 14 | **Impact check before a deletion.** Before a plan removes or renames files, routes or exports, find what still depends on them, and ask only when something does. | 16 | **Night practice.** A nightly loop within a budget re-runs the day's failed quests in fresh sandboxes against their gates; what passes review becomes a skill. | 17 | **Keys a knight uses but never sees.** A knight's script gets a placeholder, and the gateway swaps in the vault's key only on the key's pinned address. ## Already here

What the others are known for, and where Galahad has it.

| Named, persistent bots that talk to each other (Grok Bot, Hermes' bot mode) | Knights, in rooms and in direct messages between knights ✅ |
| A manager bot that coordinates the others and reports (Grok Bot) | Galahad leads every room (#1) ✅ |
| A computer per agent, with a terminal and a browser, working while you're away (Dots, Rakazo) | A KVM microVM per knight, with a desktop, a terminal and its work kept ✅ |

| Memory with full-text search (Hermes) | The mind, and the archive by words and by meaning ✅ |
| Agent CLIs behind one chat (OpenMausBot) | Knights on OpenCode, Claude Code, Codex or Hermes, each event in one stream ✅ |
| The brain kept apart from where code runs; throwaway sandboxes (Rakazo) | The node and its sealed vault, VMs and sandboxes apart ✅ |
| No two tasks on one agent's shared state at once (Rakazo's leases) | Each knight works one thing at a time, across rooms and tasks ✅ |
| Models chosen by role (Hermes) | Planner, engineer, reviewer, swift, decider, eyes and embedder ✅ (per chore: #4) |
| Typed decisions with probabilities (TypeSafe's Jev) | The decider role, through your Cloudflare AI Gateway ✅ (on your own machine: #10) |

| Open agent protocols | Galahad is an MCP server for every arm and a client of remote MCP servers, and drives Hermes over ACP ✅ |

## Declined, and why

| Idea | Why not |
|---|---|
| One shared computer whose logins every bot uses (Grok Bot) | One compromised knight would hold every session. A knight signs in on its own VM (you can take it over to do so), and Galahad on his own (#11). |
| Bots that start with full access and no sandbox (OpenMausBot's default) | Every knight works in its own VM, always. |
| A model that scores a command's blast radius and lets the safe ones through (Jev as a guardrail; Grok Bot's smarter approvals) | What needs you stays decided by the oath's classes and your word, never by a model's score. A decision model may write the risk note on a card; it never answers it. (The loop breaker in the same proposal is taken: #6.) |
| A world-state graph database kept in step with everything the agent touches | A map of the repository made by parsers as each quest starts (#14) gives the same answers with no second store to keep true. |
| A registry of tools the agent writes for itself (a just-in-time MCP forge) | Code an agent writes and later runs as one of its own tools is a channel for whatever injected it to persist, and each tool is one more way to pick wrong. Scripts in a knight's `/work`, and skills that name them, do the same job. Revisit if the trials show the same script written again and again. |
| Pheromone trails for a swarm of workers | Galahad runs a handful of knights, each in its own VM and clone, and rooms and tasks coordinate them. Revisit with many workers on one repository. |
| Speaking A2A or AG-UI to foreign agents | MCP and ACP already cover the seams Galahad has (tools, and coding agents in a knight's VM), and no foreign agent yet needs a knight to talk to it. Revisit when one does. |
| A marketplace of skills | There is no one to share them with yet. A knight exported with its skills (#20) is the first step; a skill from outside would still wait for your approval, as one does now. |
| Voice and realtime agents | Your word (14 Nov): no voice yet. And talking is the wrong axis for coding work. |
| Code as the agent's action (CodeAct) | The knights already run OpenCode, Claude Code, Codex and Hermes in their VMs, and Galahad's own loop runs one command a turn under its gates. |
| A visual flow editor | The mandate's anti-goal: not infinite configuration. A plan in words and gates that run say more than a graph of boxes. |
| Thousands of connectors as a catalog (Dots) | Remote MCP servers, added as connectors, cover the long tail with no catalog to keep. |
