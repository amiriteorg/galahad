# Learning: what the evidence says

The research behind [Learn](LEARN.md), the plan for Galahad as a tutor. On 18 November 2026 eight research agents each took one area: learning science, ADHD, autism and high ability together, motivation, AI tutoring, visuals and motion, learning products, and phones and tablets. Their notes were then condensed into the brief below. Four late-2026 claims it leans on were checked against their primary documents: the Eedi and Google trial report, the two-year Khanmigo trial, the retraction of the Wang & Fan meta-analysis, and Bartoš et al.'s meta-meta-analysis. Every other figure comes from the researchers' notes, and the strength after each finding (strong, moderate, weak, contested) is the researchers' own rating. The last paragraph names the popular claims not to quote.

## Keep the science, drop the drill

The evidence supports building "Galahad teaches" on techniques that work for nearly everyone (retrieval with explanatory feedback, spaced review, fading worked examples, and a prediction or attempt before each explanation), delivered without what this learner finds aversive: repetition of the known, evaluative pressure, opaque queues and decorative motion. Almost none of it has been tested on this learner, and no randomized trial of an AI tutor with ADHD, autistic or twice-exceptional (2e) learners was found. Guarded AI tutors avoid harm but gain modestly on broad tests (**0.06–0.08 SD a year** at best in the largest trials), and the motivation research favours tiny starts, real payoffs, the learner's own interests and rewarding the return after a lapse. The evidence backs banning decorative motion, not motion that carries the content under the learner's control. Most evidence comes from students, children and lab tasks; company data and preprints are labelled.

### Ten findings that matter most for this learner

1. **Retrieval with feedback, spaced over time, is the core.** Testing beats restudy by g = 0.50 (0.73 with feedback), and small studies of ADHD and autistic adults show the same benefit.
2. **Calibrate topic by topic and skip the known.** Extra help hurts experts (d = −0.43), boredom tracks ADHD traits (r = .40), and high IQ can hide difficulty.
3. **Predictability and control matter for autistic learners.** Intolerance of uncertainty correlates r = .62 with anxiety, sensory differences are large and individual, and autistic adults prefer written exchange; none of this is tested as a learning intervention.
4. **Starting is the bottleneck, not laziness.** Procrastination tracks task aversiveness and low expectancy; if-then plans help in general (d = .65) but are untested in adults with ADHD.
5. **Shorten the wait for a real payoff instead of adding points.** Preferring sooner rewards is robust in adults with ADHD (d = 0.40), and informative feedback beats rewards (d = 0.99 vs 0.24).
6. **Ask for a prediction before explaining.** Prequestions lift memory for what is asked (g = 0.58), and predicting a demonstration's outcome raised correct answers to 77%, against 61% with no demonstration.
7. **Interests open the door when the learner picks them and the link is deep.** Deep personalization improved adolescents' algebra learning; shallow reskins did little.
8. **Expect lapses and reward the return.** Rewarding the return after a missed workout topped 54 programmes in a 61,293-person megastudy, and self-blamed streak breaks hurt most.
9. **AI tutoring needs guardrails, and broad gains are small.** Unguarded AI cut exam scores 17%, a tutor that waited to be asked was rarely used and gave 0.06–0.08 SD a year, and about 3 in 10 supervised AI chats included a request to leave early.
10. **Ban decorative motion; keep motion that is the content, under the learner's control.** Decorative additions lower learning, while animation helps for procedures (d = 1.06) and changes that must be learned.

### 1. Retrieval and spacing lead, and feeling fluent misleads

The core techniques, with their best estimates:

Boundary conditions matter as much as averages. High-stakes quizzes erased the testing benefit ([Hinze & Rapp 2014](https://doi.org/10.1002/acp.3032)); without feedback, retrieval at or below 50% success did nothing (g = 0.03), while with feedback harder retrieval gave *larger* gains ([Rowland 2014](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0037559)). Transfer (d = 0.40) needs varied formats and application questions ([Pan & Rickard 2018](https://doi.org/10.1037/bul0000151)). In mathematics a 2025 preprint finds testing at g = 0.22 with an interval crossing zero ([Murray, Goebel & Horner 2025](https://doi.org/10.31219/osf.io/wdahf_v1)), so multi-step maths and code need worked examples alongside retrieval. Interleaving failed in a single first session ([Pan et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.1037/edu0000336)). Multimedia design principles help most with complex material ([Noetel et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.3102/00346543211052329)), though modality effects halved on re-analysis ([Reinwein 2012](https://scispace.com/papers/does-the-modality-effect-exist-and-if-so-which-modality-i7lnneq43a)) and cognitive load theory's measures remain contested ([de Jong 2010](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11251-009-9110-0)).

Learners misjudge all of this: **78% learned more from spaced study and 78% rated massed study as good or better** ([Kornell & Bjork 2008](https://bjorklab.psych.ucla.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/13/2016/07/Kornell_Bjork_2008_PsychScience.pdf)), and active physics teaching raised scores 0.46 SD while lowering the feeling of learning 0.56 SD ([Deslauriers et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1821936116)). Feedback timing is contested, with classrooms favouring immediate and labs delayed feedback ([Kulik & Kulik 1988](https://scispace.com/papers/timing-of-feedback-and-verbal-learning-14qrjt7lps)); the one adult ADHD study found learning matched controls with delayed feedback and lagged with immediate feedback, on a probabilistic lab task ([Gabay et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-018-33551-3)). No target success rate is established for complex learning; the popular 80% figure comes from correlational classroom observation ([Rosenshine 2012](https://www.aft.org/sites/default/files/periodicals/Rosenshine.pdf)). In over 20,000 game sessions, imposed difficulty made the easiest levels most motivating and chosen difficulty favoured moderate ones ([Lomas et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.1145/3025453.3025638)).

The neurodivergent evidence is thin but consistent. College students with ADHD practising retrieval to criterion matched peers a week later ([Knouse et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.learninstruc.2020.101330)); with word pairs, the testing benefit did not depend on ADHD status but did not offset lower encoding in unmedicated adults ([Minear et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2023.1186566)); one prose study found no benefit ([Dudukovic et al. 2015](https://doi.org/10.1080/09658211.2014.977921)). Autistic adults learning word pairs gained from retrieval and feedback as non-autistic adults did ([Cairney et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.1177/13623613261464659)). No study was found testing interleaving, worked examples, self-explanation, multimedia design or productive failure with ADHD or autistic adults, or any technique with 2e learners (weak overall).

### 2. ADHD: delay and boredom are robust, while popular frameworks are untested

Profiles vary: average executive-function differences are moderate (d = 0.46–0.69 across 83 studies) but "neither necessary nor sufficient" for ADHD ([Willcutt et al. 2005](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15950006/)), and tests barely track everyday ratings (median r = .19) ([Toplak et al. 2013](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23057693/)) (strong). No experiment on task initiation in adults with ADHD was found; in 202 adults surveyed online, ADHD traits related to procrastination through low expectancy of success and impulsiveness ([Netzer Turgeman & Pollak 2023](https://doi.org/10.1080/00050067.2023.2218540)) (weak to moderate).

The robust adult finding concerns delay: people with ADHD more often choose smaller, sooner rewards (**d = 0.40 in adults**) in lab choice tasks ([Jackson & MacKillop 2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5049699)) (strong). Evidence that rewards boost performance comes from children's lab tasks ([Luman et al. 2005](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cpr.2004.11.001)); in adults it is contested, as reward normalised one timing measure ([Marx et al. 2013](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23840573/)) and in another study did nothing except raise self-reported hyperactivity ([Kallweit et al. 2021](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31096844/)). Boredom proneness correlates **r = 0.40** with ADHD traits across 18 mostly correlational studies ([Muris et al. 2026](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41811543/)) (strong as an association) and tracks low information gain ([Seiler et al. 2025](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40128552/)), which makes repeating known material a likely trigger. Novelty seeking is raised in children, but adult personality meta-analyses point to low conscientiousness instead ([Jacobsson et al. 2021](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34074265/)). No ADHD trial of session length or breaks was found.

The "interest-based nervous system" is a clinical framing that cites no study ([Dodson, ADDitude](https://www.additudemag.com/secrets-of-the-adhd-brain/)), and no peer-reviewed test was found (weak). Hyperfocus, measured by questionnaire, correlates r = .53 with ADHD traits but only r = .12 with flow ([Hupfeld et al. 2024](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39169147/)); diagnosed adults hyperfocused no more than matched controls, and less in education ([Groen et al. 2020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33126147/)); and it predicts burnout ([Pyszkowska et al. 2026](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41650538/)) (contested as a strength). Body doubling is popular (75% of 220 mostly neurodivergent survey respondents, per a secondary summary) ([Eagle et al. 2024](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3689648)), but its only controlled test, AI double included, is a 12-person virtual-reality preprint ([Ara et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.12153)) (weak). If-then plans are strong in general (d = .65) ([Gollwitzer & Sheeran 2006](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0065-2601%2806%2938002-1)) and helped children with ADHD in the lab ([Gawrilow & Gollwitzer 2008](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10608-007-9150-1)), but no adult ADHD trial was found; guided planning beat written templates ([Wang, Wang & Gai 2021](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2021.565202/full)).

Irrelevant salient images distracted adults with ADHD more in the lab, and a perceptually demanding task reduced the extra cost ([Forster et al. 2014](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24219607/)), though a population study tied distractibility only weakly to ADHD traits ([Meier 2021](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33709382/)) (moderate). Adult CBT improves self-rated symptoms (g = 0.65) ([Knouse et al. 2017](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28504540/)) (moderate), but internet CBT did not beat relaxation training ([Nasri et al. 2023](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37483263/)) and only 29% completed every module of a self-guided programme ([Kenter et al. 2023](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36969389/)). Cognitive training does not transfer to blinded ratings or academics ([Westwood et al. 2023](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36977764/)) (strong), EndeavorRx's FDA authorisation covers only computer-measured attention in children ([FDA DEN200026](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/cdrh_docs/reviews/DEN200026.pdf)), and no randomized trial of gamified learning for adults with ADHD was found.

### 3. Autism: autistic adults' own accounts outrun the trials

Monotropism, an autistic-developed theory of attention drawn into a few deep channels ([Murray, Lesser & Lawson 2005](https://doi.org/10.1177/1362361305051398)), has a questionnaire only in a 2023 preprint whose scores also rise with ADHD ([Garau et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.31219/osf.io/ft73y)), so its empirical support is weak. Hyperfocus is raised in autistic, ADHD and autistic-ADHD adults alike and tied both to anxiety and to positive experiences ([Dwyer et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1177/27546330241237883)) (moderate). Inertia, an involuntary difficulty starting, stopping and switching, is described consistently: adults in focus groups said prompting and a compatible activity helped them start ([Buckle et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2021.631596)), and interviewees called it "the single most disabling part of being Autistic" while prizing immersion ([Rapaport et al. 2024](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37776056/)). That is strong as testimony and weak as measurement.

About two-thirds of autistic adults report an intense interest; engagement goes with higher wellbeing, very high intensity with lower ([Grove et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.1002/aur.1931)) (moderate, correlational). All 20 studies of teaching autistic children through their interests reported gains, likely inflated by publication bias, and none involved adults ([Gunn & Delafield-Butt 2016](https://doi.org/10.3102/0034654315604027)). Intolerance of uncertainty correlates **r = 0.62** with anxiety ([Jenkinson et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1177/1362361320932437)) and links sensory sensitivity to anxiety in autistic adults ([Hwang et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1177/1362361319868907)) (moderate), while TEACCH-style structured teaching has only small, exploratory support ([Virués-Ortega et al. 2013](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cpr.2013.07.005)) (weak). Sensory differences are large and individual across 55 studies ([Ben-Sasson et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10803-019-04180-0)) (strong), but no study of on-screen animation with autistic adults was found.

Figurative-language comprehension is lower on average (g = −0.57), but the gap becomes small and non-significant when groups are matched on language ability ([Kalandadze et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.1177/1362361316668652)) (moderate to strong), so precise wording helps without implying that autistic adults cannot handle idiom. The double empathy problem ([Milton 2012](https://doi.org/10.1080/09687599.2012.710008)) is contested: a 2025 registered report (N = 311) found no extra information loss in mixed-neurotype exchanges, though rapport differed ([Crompton et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41562-025-02163-z)). Camouflaging tracks worse mental health in cross-sectional data ([Cook et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cpr.2021.102080)), and autistic adults prefer email with strangers and dislike phone calls ([Howard & Sedgewick 2021](https://doi.org/10.1177/13623613211014995)) (both moderate). Eleven autistic workers strongly preferred an LLM to a human for workplace questions while a specialist coach judged some of its advice questionable ([Jang et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1145/3613904.3642894)) (weak). PDA is not a diagnosis ([PDA Society](https://www.pdasociety.org.uk/what-is-pda/)), its research rests on parent reports ([Kildahl et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1177/13623613211034382)), and in community adults demand-avoidant traits tracked ADHD traits (r = 0.71) far more than autistic ones ([Egan, Bull & Trundle 2020](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ridd.2020.103733)) (contested). Offering choices and explaining purpose is the low-risk response, though untested as such.

### 4. High ability and 2e: boredom signals a task problem, and ability hides difficulty

In five university studies, low perceived control and value predicted boredom, which predicted attention problems and lower effort and performance ([Pekrun et al. 2010](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0019243)) (moderate to strong). Under-challenge as the cause of gifted boredom is weaker and mixed: gifted ninth graders in regular classes were no more bored than those in gifted classes ([Preckel, Goetz & Frenzel 2010](https://doi.org/10.1348/000709909X480716)), while disengaged gifted teens named control, choice, challenge, complexity and caring teachers ([Kanevsky & Keighley 2003](https://doi.org/10.1080/02783190309554235), qualitative). Programmes for gifted underachievers do not move grades (g = 0.09) ([Steenbergen-Hu et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1177/0016986220908601)).

No fixed "why before how" order wins: iterating concepts and procedures matched or beat concepts-first in children ([Rittle-Johnson & Koedinger 2009](https://doi.org/10.1348/000709908x398106)), and IQ has not been shown to moderate ordering effects (moderate, contested). Acceleration is strongly supported in school (**g = 0.70** against same-age peers) ([Steenbergen-Hu, Makel & Olszewski-Kubilius 2016](https://doi.org/10.3102/0034654316675417)), with no wellbeing cost at age 50 ([Bernstein, Lubinski & Benbow 2021](https://doi.org/10.1037/edu0000500)), but self-paced mastery programmes "often reduce the completion rates in college classes" ([Kulik et al. 1990](https://doi.org/10.3102/00346543060002265)).

Perfectionistic concerns correlate r = .23 with procrastination and strivings r = −.22 ([Sirois, Molnar & Hirsch 2017](https://doi.org/10.1002/per.2098)) (strong); gifted college students trace self-oriented perfectionism to early effortless success and no experience of failure ([Speirs Neumeister 2004](https://doi.org/10.1177/001698620404800402), qualitative), and whether gifted people are more perfectionistic is unverified. Mindset interventions average d = 0.05, non-significant after bias correction ([Macnamara & Burgoyne 2023](https://doi.org/10.1037/bul0000352)), with no effect for higher-achieving ninth graders in a national trial ([Yeager et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-019-1466-y)) (contested). Asynchrony is largely untested theory ([Silverman 1997](https://doi.org/10.1080/0161956X.1997.9681865)), but profiles are uneven: under 5% of top-1% students had flat ones ([Achter, Lubinski & Benbow 1996](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-0167.43.1.65)), and high IQ goes with less anxiety, not more ([Williams et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1192/j.eurpsy.2022.2343)). Twice-exceptionality is real: high-IQ adults with ADHD show the same difficulties as other adults with ADHD ([Antshel et al. 2009](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0033291708004959)), higher IQ hides executive differences on tests ([Milioni et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.1177/1087054714554933)), and high-ability autistic youth self-report average scores where parents and teachers see difficulty ([Foley Nicpon et al. 2010](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10803-010-0952-8)) (moderate). Research on what helps 2e learners is mostly descriptive and child-based ([Gelbar et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1177/00169862211061876)) (weak).

### 5. Motivation: value and return beat pressure and points

Autonomous motivation (interest plus endorsed value) goes with persistence and wellbeing, while guilt-driven motivation buys persistence at a wellbeing cost ([Howard et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1177/1745691620966789)) (strong, correlational). Rewards for merely doing a task undermined later free-choice engagement (d = −0.40) while informational feedback raised it (d = 0.33), mostly in lab studies and more in children ([Deci, Koestner & Ryan 1999](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.125.6.627)); critics confine the harm to expected tangible rewards ([Cameron & Pierce 1994](https://scispace.com/papers/reinforcement-reward-and-intrinsic-motivation-a-meta-38uaaciurt)) (moderate). Choice helps modestly, less in adults, best with two to four options ([Patall, Cooper & Robinson 2008](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.134.2.270)). Value-writing interventions helped low-expectancy high schoolers ([Hulleman & Harackiewicz 2009](https://scispace.com/papers/promoting-interest-and-performance-in-high-school-science-th3rc2er2y)) but shrank by an order of magnitude across about 250,000 online learners ([Kizilcec et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1921417117)) (moderate).

Deep interest personalization made ninth graders faster and more accurate in an algebra tutor, with effects carrying to later problems and exams ([Walkington 2013](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0031882); [Bernacki & Walkington 2018](https://doi.org/10.1037/edu0000250)); coarse interest categories did not help ([Fancsali & Ritter 2014](https://scispace.com/papers/context-personalization-preferences-and-performance-in-an-2i5agqsmcr)), and generative-AI personalization raised adults' motivation but not learning ([Leong et al. 2024](https://scispace.com/papers/putting-things-into-context-generative-ai-enabled-context-4ydc7hdaxj)) (moderate in adolescents, weak in adults). In adult lab trivia, curiosity predicted recall weeks later ([Kang et al. 2009](https://scispace.com/papers/the-wick-in-the-candle-of-learning-epistemic-curiosity-5crbxbsh33)), and a bare "correct" nearly extinguished curiosity while a confident error heightened it ([Metcalfe et al. 2023](https://scispace.com/papers/curiosity-the-effects-of-feedback-and-confidence-on-the-1rfcgc1j)) (moderate). Challenge–skill balance relates only moderately to flow ([Fong et al. 2015](https://doi.org/10.1080/17439760.2014.967799)) (contested as a design rule), and boredom signals either a demand mismatch, too easy or too hard, or a meaning mismatch ([Westgate & Wilson 2018](https://scispace.com/papers/boring-thoughts-and-bored-minds-the-mac-model-of-boredom-and-4p6tz8hnsa)).

Procrastination follows task aversiveness, delay, low self-efficacy and impulsiveness ([Steel 2007](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.133.1.65)) and works as short-term mood repair ([Sirois & Pychyl 2013](https://scispace.com/papers/procrastination-and-the-priority-of-short-term-mood-w0alj9zxav)), not laziness (strong for the correlates). Behavioural activation (acting before motivation arrives) is strong for depression but untested for learning ([Cuijpers et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cpr.2026.102783)). Habit automaticity took 18 to 254 days and one miss did not matter ([Lally et al. 2010](https://doi.org/10.1002/ejsp.674)) (moderate), and no peer-reviewed trial of the Tiny Habits method was found (weak).

Streaks raise engagement because keeping them becomes a goal; breaks hurt more when self-attributed and less when repairable ([Silverman & Barasch 2023](https://doi.org/10.1093/jcr/ucac029)) (moderate). Duolingo reports +3.3% day-14 retention from a streak redesign and +0.38% daily learners from streak freezes, without sample sizes ([Duolingo 2020](https://blog.duolingo.com/improving-the-streak); [Duolingo 2022](https://blog.duolingo.com/how-duolingo-streak-builds-habit)) (company-reported, weak), and critics document dependency rather than habit ([Renfree et al. 2016](https://scispace.com/papers/don-t-kick-the-habit-the-role-of-dependency-in-habit-gqopj3kufu); [Maaß 2026](https://scispace.com/papers/encouraging-repeated-app-usage-through-habit-forming-p6g0y6ge0r7u)). Loss aversion is weaker than folklore: λ ≈ 1.8–2.1 pooled across designs ([Brown et al. 2024](https://scispace.com/papers/meta-analysis-of-empirical-estimates-of-loss-aversion-273mghjn4h)) but 1.31 in risky choice ([Walasek, Mullett & Stewart 2024](https://scispace.com/papers/a-meta-analysis-of-loss-aversion-in-risky-contexts-2nyvvr4907)) (contested). Gamification barely moves intrinsic motivation (g = 0.26) ([Li, Hew & Du 2024](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11423-023-10337-7)), and a leaderboard-and-badges course section lost motivation over 16 weeks ([Hanus & Fox 2015](https://scispace.com/papers/assessing-the-effects-of-gamification-in-the-classroom-58wwwueamc)) (moderate). Random rewards failed where rewards for learning behaviour raised persistence ([O'Rourke et al. 2016](https://scispace.com/papers/brain-points-a-deeper-look-at-a-growth-mindset-incentive-48hwogh8mm)) (weak), and the EU AI Act bans AI that exploits disability-related vulnerabilities in harmful ways ([AI Act Art. 5](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/5/)). In a 61,293-person gym megastudy, **the best of 54 programmes rewarded returning after a missed workout, only 8% had lasting effects, and experts could not pick the winners** ([Milkman et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-021-04128-4)) (moderate); self-set deadlines help but are set badly ([Ariely & Wertenbroch 2002](https://scispace.com/papers/procrastination-deadlines-and-performance-self-control-by-rkegifhvai)).

### 6. AI tutoring: guardrails prevent harm, and broad gains stay small

Bloom's "two sigma" came from two three-week experiments with schoolchildren, narrow tests and bundled mastery testing ([von Hippel 2024](https://www.educationnext.org/two-sigma-tutoring-separating-science-fiction-from-science/), a magazine essay re-reading the dissertations) (strong critique). K–12 tutoring RCTs pool to 0.29 SD ([Nickow et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.3102/00028312231208687)), and preregistered federal trials have a median of 0.03 SD; for field trials on standardized tests, Kraft calls under 0.05 SD small, 0.05 to 0.20 medium and 0.20 or more large ([Kraft 2020](https://annenberg.brown.edu/publications/interpreting-effect-sizes-education-interventions)). Intelligent tutoring systems nearly match human tutors (d = 0.76 vs 0.79) ([VanLehn 2011](https://doi.org/10.1080/00461520.2011.611369)).

A heavily scaffolded GPT-4 tutor with expert-written solutions beat active-learning physics classes on immediate tests in one Harvard course (N = 194): 0.63 SD by linear regression, which the authors call an underestimate because of a ceiling, and 0.73–1.3 SD by quantile regression, in less time (median 49 vs 60 minutes). A prompt alone could not scaffold multi-part problems ([Kestin et al. 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12179260/)) (moderate). Among about 1,000 Turkish high schoolers, unguarded GPT-4 raised practice scores 48% and **cut unassisted exam scores 17%**, while a guarded tutor raised practice 127% with no exam gain ([Bastani et al. 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12232635/)) (strong for the harm). In the Eedi and Google trial (a preregistered company report, not peer-reviewed; 1,179 students in 10 UK schools for 10 weeks, every message supervised), AI matched expert human tutors on near measures, withheld answers until an attempt in 98.8% of chats and kept each kind of error under 3 in 1,000 messages, but produced no Star Maths gain over standard remediation, slowed coverage, and drew requests to leave early in about 3 in 10 chats ([LearnLM Team & Eedi 2026](https://services.google.com/fh/files/misc/learnlm_oct26.pdf)) (moderate). The independent two-year Khanmigo trial in 18 Tennessee middle schools (a working paper) found **0.06–0.08 SD a year** (the low end of Kraft's medium band), similar to Khan Academy practice without AI; the median student messaged the tutor in 17% of exercise sessions with a mistake, mostly with bare answers ([Oreopoulos & Low 2026](https://edworkingpapers.com/sites/default/files/ai26-1551.pdf)) (strong for modest effects and low use).

A ChatGPT meta-analysis claiming g = 0.867 was retracted in May 2026 ([Plagiarism Today](https://www.plagiarismtoday.com/2026/07/15/study-claiming-ai-helps-students-learn-retracted/)); a meta-meta-analysis of 1,840 effects from 67 meta-analyses (a preprint) gives a bias-adjusted **SMD of 0.196 (95% CrI 0.000–0.323)** ([Bartoš et al. 2026](https://sciety.org/articles/activity/10.31234/osf.io/h529e_v1)) (contested field). Among 52 engineers, AI help cut later unaided quiz scores from 67% to 50% without significant time savings ([Shen & Tamkin 2026](https://www.anthropic.com/research/AI-assistance-coding-skills), company-affiliated), while LLM explanations helped adults most after an attempt ([Kumar et al.](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=4641653)) (moderate).

Constructive work, not Socratic form, is the active ingredient: tutors barred from explaining did as well by prompting ([Chi et al. 2001](https://dblp.uni-trier.de/rec/journals/cogsci/ChiSJYH01.html)), and dialogue beat reading only when material was above the learner's level ([VanLehn et al. 2007](https://doi.org/10.1080/03640210709336984)) (moderate). How much help to give remains an "assistance dilemma" ([Koedinger & Aleven 2007](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-007-9049-0)); teaching help-seeking changed help use but not learning ([Aleven et al. 2016](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40593-015-0089-1)), and boredom, not frustration, preceded gaming the hints ([Baker et al. 2010](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijhcs.2009.12.003)) (moderate). The evidence supports withholding the answer until a real attempt and then escalating, not never revealing it. GPT-4 revealed answers about 47% of the time in a tutoring benchmark ([Maurya et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.09416)), and models caved to claimed authority in an early preprint ([Kasneci & Kasneci 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.14604)) (weak). The neurodivergent evidence is a three-child single-case study ([Mautone et al. 2005](https://doi.org/10.1177/1087054705278832)) and co-design with 20 university students with ADHD ([Zhu et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.09381)) (weak), and no independent evaluation of any vendor's study mode was found.

### 7. Visuals: predict-then-reveal works, and motion earns its place only as content

Explorable explanations rest on design essays ([Victor 2011](https://worrydream.com/ExplorableExplanations/)) and "limited empirical evaluation" ([Hohman et al. 2020](https://distill.pub/2020/communicating-with-interactive-articles/)) (weak as a genre). Interactivity helps when it forces a commitment: adults who drew a predicted trend before seeing the data recalled and understood more ([Kim, Reinecke & Hullman 2017](https://idl.uw.edu/papers/explaining-the-gap)), while interaction for its own sake cut accuracy ([Mosca, Ottley & Chang 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.01701v2)), and university students left interactive pictures unused unless prompted, spent longer for equal scores and still preferred them ([Zhao et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1111/test.12324)). Simulations beat none (K–12 g = 0.62; higher education g = 0.85) ([D'Angelo et al. 2014](https://www.sri.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/12/simulations-for-stem-learning-executive-summary.pdf); [Chernikova et al. 2020](https://portal.fis.tum.de/en/publications/simulation-based-learning-in-higher-education-a-meta-analysis/)), but unassisted discovery loses to explicit instruction (d = −0.38) while guided discovery wins (d = 0.30) ([Alfieri et al. 2011](https://www.fisme.science.uu.nl/publicaties/literatuur/2011_alfieri_discovery_learning.pdf)) (moderate to strong), and learner control alone averages g = 0.05 ([Karich, Burns & Maki 2014](https://experts.umn.edu/en/publications/updated-meta-analysis-of-learner-control-within-educational-techn/fingerprints/)). In a university physics class, students who predicted a demonstration's outcome got 77% right, against 70% for watching and 61% with no demonstration, for about two extra minutes ([Crouch et al. 2004](https://www.otffeo.on.ca/en/wp-content/uploads/sites/2/2014/11/Mazur_demo-article.pdf)) (moderate), a benefit that in children depended on executive function ([Brod et al. 2020](https://www.pedocs.de/volltexte/2021/23315/pdf/Developmental_Science_2020_3_Brod_et_al_Being_proven_wrong_A.pdf)).

Drawing what a text describes helps (g = 0.69; factual 0.85, transfer 0.22), mostly against weak controls ([Cromley, Du & Dane 2020](https://scispace.com/papers/drawing-to-learn-does-meta-analysis-show-differences-between-39k8k58prr)) (moderate); students rarely choose it ([Eidman & Fiorella 2024](https://scispace.com/papers/why-dont-students-draw-when-learning-from-science-texts-3jx4pj9aad)), and finger drawing beat a digital pencil for recall in two tablet studies ([Xie et al. 2023](https://scispace.com/papers/finger-versus-pencil-an-eye-tracking-study-of-learning-by-3m1wgyc181); [Xie et al. 2024](https://scispace.com/papers/investigating-the-effect-of-drawing-medium-on-learning-6g487i1ef7fx)) (weak). The handwriting-over-typing claim failed direct replication ([Urry et al. 2021](https://scispace.com/papers/don-t-ditch-the-laptop-just-yet-a-direct-replication-of-1je6592y4o)), though a 2024 meta-analysis of 24 college studies found a small advantage for taking and reviewing handwritten notes (g = 0.25) ([Flanigan et al. 2024](https://scholars.georgiasouthern.edu/en/publications/typed-versus-handwritten-lecture-notes-and-college-student-achiev/)) (contested). Audits of AI-generated physics diagrams find missing or misdirected vectors, worsening with complexity ([Fulminar 2026](https://scispace.com/papers/an-aid-or-not-examining-chatgpt-generated-visualizations-in-d7ug1hw1apyv)) (weak).

#### Motion: what the evidence supports banning, and where it helps

Animation beats static pictures only slightly on average (d = 0.37 in [Höffler & Leutner 2007](https://www.leibniz-ipn.de/en/research/publications/instructional-animation-versus-static-pictures-a-meta-analysis); g = 0.23 in [Berney & Bétrancourt 2016](https://archive-ouverte.unige.ch/unige:92234)), and much apparent superiority comes from animations that carried more information than their static comparisons ([Tversky, Morrison & Bétrancourt 2002](https://hci.stanford.edu/courses/cs448b/papers/Tversky_AnimationFacilitate_IJHCS02.pdf)).

| Kind of motion | What the evidence supports | Strength |
|---|---|---|
| Decorative or ambient: spinners, pulsing, shimmer, bouncing, parallax, animated mascots | **Ban.** Motion onset captures attention involuntarily ([Abrams & Christ 2003](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12930472/)); irrelevant additions lower learning, g ≈ −0.33 per a secondary summary ([Sundararajan & Adesope 2020](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-020-09522-4)); salient distractors interfere more for adults with ADHD ([Forster et al. 2014](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24219607/)); autistic adults keep processing irrelevant stimuli at higher loads ([Remington et al. 2009](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/journals/psychological-science/j.1467-9280.2009.02454.x/)); motion can cause dizziness and nausea ([WCAG 2.3.3](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/animation-from-interactions.html)) | Moderate; no study tests UI spinners on learning, so the ban rests on mechanism |
| Autoplaying, system-paced animation of explanations | **Ban as a default.** Static diagrams with text matched or beat narrated animation in four university experiments ([Mayer et al. 2005](https://doi.org/10.1037/1076-898X.11.4.256)); animated trends were worst for analysis ([Robertson et al. 2008](https://faculty.cc.gatech.edu/~stasko/papers/infovis08-anim.pdf)); transient content outruns working memory ([Castro-Alonso, Ayres & Paas 2016](https://ro.uow.edu.au/sspapers/2673)) | Moderate |
| Motion that is the content: procedures, human movement, how a change unfolds | **Keep, started, paused and scrubbed by the learner.** Procedural-motor d = 1.06 ([Höffler & Leutner 2007](https://www.leibniz-ipn.de/en/research/publications/instructional-animation-versus-static-pictures-a-meta-analysis)); when change must be learned, g = 0.34 simple and 0.65 complex ([Ploetzner et al. 2021](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11251-021-09541-w)); learner pacing beat continuous playback for children ([Hasler et al. 2007](https://core.ac.uk/works/19653879)), though biology students did better system-paced ([Koc-Januchta et al. 2020](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/s11423-020-09822-0.pdf)) | Moderate to strong |
| Figures that redraw as the learner drags a control | **Keep.** Supported by the guided-simulation evidence, not the animation literature | Moderate; attention and vestibular effects untested |

Warmth does not need motion: emotional-design benefits come from static colour and friendly faces (retention d = 0.39) ([Brom, Stárková & D'Mello 2018](https://artemis.ms.mff.cuni.cz/main/papers/emodesign_manuscript_1rev_180928_FINAL_norev.pdf)) and are reportedly stronger with static than animated materials ([Wong & Adesope 2021](https://rex.libraries.wsu.edu/esploro/outputs/journalArticle/Meta-Analysis-of-Emotional-Designs-in-Multimedia/99900601054701842), secondary).

### 8. Products: each gets something right and misses this learner somewhere

| Product | Right for this learner | Wrong for this learner | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Math Academy | Problems within minutes, tiny steps, halts after repeated errors, review credit when later topics use earlier ones ([company](https://www.mathacademy.com/how-our-ai-works)) | Motivation built on chores and negative XP ([company book](https://justinmath.com/files/the-math-academy-way.pdf)); expert users found the queue opaque and the teaching "unabashedly procedural" ([Matuschak](https://notes.andymatuschak.org/Math_Academy); [Pershan](https://pershmail.substack.com/p/math-academy-wants-to-supercharge?open=false), anecdote) | Company only; "don't have any official academic studies" ([Skycak](https://www.justinmath.com/individualized-spaced-repetition-in-hierarchical-knowledge-structures/)) |
| Brilliant | Problem-first, visual; its 2026 tutor Koji speaks first and withholds answers ([company](https://blog.brilliant.org/a-world-class-tutor-in-every-home/)) | Weekly leagues; default voice may clash with a preference for text (inference) | No efficacy data; "6x" claim's source unconfirmed ([CMU](https://www.cmu.edu//news//stories/archives/2015/september/moocs-vs-oli.html)) |
| Duolingo | Very short sessions, a fitted memory model, candid A/B reporting | A better scheduler cut practice 7.3% without raising engagement; learners practised "to keep the tree gold" ([Settles & Meeder 2016](https://aclanthology.org/P16-1174.pdf)) | Company studies of completers ([Jiang et al. 2021](https://experts.nau.edu/en/publications/evaluating-the-reading-and-listening-outcomes-of-beginning-level-/)); one independent study, n = 9 ([Loewen et al. 2019](https://cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/A4D7C8F71782A37D258C19F357DDBCBE/S0958344019000065a.pdf/mobileassisted_language_learning_a_duolingo_case_study.pdf)) |
| Anki with FSRS | One dial, desired retention; FSRS-6 predicts recall better than SM-2 for 99.6% of users ([Expertium](https://expertium.github.io/Benchmark.html); [benchmark](https://github.com/open-spaced-repetition/srs-benchmark)) | Assumes daily discipline, so breaks become backlogs ([Anki manual](https://docs.ankiweb.net/deck-options.html)) | Prediction strong; review savings unverified; outcomes correlational ([Deng et al. 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4673073)) |
| Khan Academy, Khanmigo | Mastery checks; focused practice gave 0.22 SD at one randomized site ([J-PAL](https://www.povertyactionlab.org/evaluation/impact-teaching-teachers-use-computer-assisted-learning-student-math-performance-united)) | About 9% reach the recommended dose ([company](https://blog.khanacademy.org/?p=18194)); help-on-request AI underdelivered ([Sal Khan](https://blog.khanacademy.org/khanmigos-first-chapter-changed-how-i-think-about-ai-a-note-from-sal-khan/)) | One RCT with a null second site; company quasi-experiments |
| Synthesis Tutor | Manipulatives and mastery checks for children | Cites another program's Navy results as evidence ([company](https://synthesis.com/blog/does-the-synthesis-tutor-get-results)) | Marketing only |
| Quantum Country | Prompts inside a narrative; for readers who kept reviewing to the sixth repetition, about 95 minutes of review bought around 54 days of demonstrated retention per question ([Matuschak & Nielsen](https://numinous.productions/ttft/)) | 29% of readers who finished the in-essay prompts reached the one-month level; author-imposed prompts ([Matuschak](https://notes.andymatuschak.org/zqetsQERteJhyPitmRoaeE)) | Self-selected internal data; no transfer evidence |

Three lessons recur. **Dosage is the binding constraint**, so each session must be worth having alone. **Incentives get gamed**: Math Academy describes "XP hackers" who guess to fail out of harder tasks (company), and Duolingo learners served the meter. **Help must be woven into the task**: the AI "could not just sit next to the content," in Sal Khan's words. Unsupported efficacy claims carry legal risk, as Lumosity's $2 million FTC settlement showed ([FTC 2016](https://ftc.gov/news-events/press-releases/2016/01/lumosity-pay-2-million-settle-ftc-deceptive-advertising-charges)).

### 9. Phone and tablet: seconds on the phone, minutes on the iPad, and a still screen

Mobile-learning meta-analyses report g = 0.67 ([Yang & Xiang 2024](https://doi.org/10.1177/07356331241226589)), but an RCT-only review of primary pupils found d = 0.24 ([Dorris 2026](https://doi.org/10.17034/32633385), repository record): the activity matters more than the device (moderate). App sessions averaged 72 seconds ([Böhmer et al. 2011](https://www.brenthecht.com/papers/bhecht_mobilehci2011_sleepbirds.pdf)), and median unlocked sessions ran **1.2 minutes on phones vs 3.3 on tablets** ([Hintze et al. 2014](https://ins.jku.at/publications/2014/Hintze_2014_USmile.pdf)), from old Android logs. Paper beats screens slightly for expository text (g = −0.21), more under time pressure ([Delgado et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.edurev.2018.09.003)), and the gap is smaller on handhelds (g = −0.11) ([Salmerón et al. 2024](https://dx.doi.org/10.1037/edu0000830)) (strong); screen readers are overconfident ([Clinton 2019](https://doi.org/10.1111/1467-9817.12269)), as were students with ADHD on digital text ([Ben-Yehudah & Brann 2019](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30981195/)).

Notifications create near-term starts (a 3.5-fold rise in app opening within the hour) without changing time to disengagement ([Bell et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.2196/38342)) (strong for starts, weak for retention), and their effect fades with repetition ([Klasnja et al. 2019](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6401341)); Duolingo's bandit gained 0.5% daily users and penalised repeated wording ([Yancey & Settles 2020](https://research.duolingo.com/papers/yancey.kdd20.pdf), company-authored). Relevance predicted receptivity more than timing ([Fischer et al. 2010](https://scispace.com/papers/effects-of-content-and-time-of-delivery-on-receptivity-to-2rpejwg05r)). A week with alerts on raised 221 students' self-reported inattention and hyperactivity. The paper reports d ≈ 0.44, but in raw terms the change is about a tenth of a scale point, roughly 0.2 SD by our calculation from its means, not the authors' ([Kushlev, Proulx & Dunn 2016](https://www.interruptions.net/literature/Kushlev-CHI16.pdf)) (moderate, for a small effect). Apple reserves Time Sensitive alerts for events within the hour ([HIG](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/managing-notifications)). Glanceable-display evidence comes only from activity tracking ([Gouveia et al. 2015](https://scispace.com/papers/how-do-we-engage-with-activity-trackers-a-longitudinal-study-4kkdqo1ruk)) (weak); no study links iOS widgets to learning.

Handwritten algebra entry by school students was faster and less error-prone than typing, with similar learning in less time ([Anthony, Yang & Koedinger 2007](https://scispace.com/papers/benefits-of-handwritten-input-for-students-learning-algebra-4mor0jay21)) but a null elsewhere ([de Morais et al. 2017](https://scispace.com/papers/the-use-of-handwriting-input-in-math-tutoring-systems-an-use-59b6b0uwhe)) (moderate for entry, weak for learning), and less paper-like pen interfaces raised cognitive load ([Oviatt, Arthur & Cohen 2006](https://scispace.com/papers/quiet-interfaces-that-help-students-think-1wnq6tqwld)); Math Notes is unevaluated. Haptics show little learning benefit ([Sheshadri et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1145/3313831.3376272)) (weak), over half of autistic youth are sensory over-responsive ([Green et al. 2015](https://scispace.com/papers/neurobiology-of-sensory-overresponsivity-in-youth-with-5788ttg1mp)), and Apple says to make haptics optional ([HIG](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/playing-haptics)). Apple's guidance says "Don't add motion for the sake of adding motion" ([HIG](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/motion)); WCAG requires a pause control for auto-moving content over five seconds, naming people with attention differences ([WCAG 2.2.2](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/pause-stop-hide.html)), and at most three flashes a second ([WCAG 2.3.1](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/three-flashes-or-below-threshold.html)). Vestibular dysfunction appears in 35.4% of US adults aged 40 and over on a balance test ([Agrawal et al. 2009](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamainternalmedicine/articlepdf/773517/ioi90006_938_944.pdf)) (strong for the prevalence; how many of them on-screen motion bothers is unmeasured), and Reduce Motion adoption is not public, so a still default protects people who never find the setting. Dyslexia fonts ([Kuster et al. 2018](https://scispace.com/papers/dyslexie-font-does-not-benefit-reading-in-children-with-or-1c9vl8mz9t)) do not help (strong null, tested mostly in children), and neither does Bionic Reading for skilled readers ([Parker et al. 2026](https://scispace.com/papers/does-the-selective-bolding-of-characters-within-words-h6k617bw4t0b)) (moderate null; a few small studies with second-language readers or readers with reading disabilities report gains); text-to-speech helps readers with reading difficulties (d = 0.35) ([Wood et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.1177/0022219416688170)) (moderate).

### What the evidence says Galahad should do, and must never do

#### Should do

Make **low-stakes retrieval with explanatory feedback the default**, spaced to the learner's horizon, and judge success by delayed, unassisted performance, not in-session ease (strong). **Assess prior knowledge topic by topic**, skip what is known, allow acceleration, and fully guide unfamiliar topics whatever the learner's IQ, fading support as accuracy rises (strong). **Get a prediction or attempt, explain by building on it, check briefly, then stop** once understanding shows (moderate). **Ground every judgment in a verified solution, executed code or a checked figure** (strong for the risk).

**Make starting tiny and concrete**, with a brief if-then plan made in conversation; be proactive at the start, quiet during immersion, and offer stopping points (moderate to weak). **Shorten the delay to a real payoff**, such as code that runs (strong for the preference), and **teach through interests the learner chooses** (moderate in adolescents). **Say what comes next and for how long, offer two to four real options, define terms, stay text-first and accept the learner's communication style** (moderate). **Treat lapses as normal**: re-entry without guilt, the return rewarded, review budgeted in minutes (moderate). Keep visuals static or learner-stepped with one prompted manipulation at a time (moderate). Keep phone moments to a minute or two and save long work for the iPad, keep nudges rare, opted-in and varied, and keep haptics optional and off by default (moderate).

#### Must never do

Never pad sessions with **repetition of mastered material**, or make **the full answer the default route to "done"**. Never present unverified output or diagrams as correct, or abandon a correct judgment under pushback. Never treat **practice scores, XP, engagement or preference as learning**, and never make quizzes high-stakes or reading timed. Never use **guilt, loss-framed streaks, default leaderboards, negative points, chance-based rewards or personalised reinforcement loops** to maximise time in the app, an ethical and, in the EU, a legal risk for a population defined partly by disability. Never use **decorative or autoplaying motion, flashing or strong default haptics**, or carry essential information only in motion or vibration. Never send nudges as Time Sensitive or Critical alerts, in repeated wording or mid-session. Never **diagnose or label** anyone, moralise about laziness, or coach toward neurotypical communication. Never claim to **treat ADHD, train attention or deliver two-sigma gains**. Never interrupt productive immersion for engagement mechanics, or turn interests into rewards to be earned (an inference from child studies).

### What the evidence does not settle

The largest gap is the learner: **no controlled study has tested an AI tutor with ADHD, autistic or 2e adults or older teens**, and beyond retrieval practice almost no technique has been tested with them. For tutoring, it is unknown whether supervised AI's near-term gains become broad, durable learning, when to stop questioning a fast or confident learner (in the Eedi team's words, "a crucial research challenge"), whether ADHD learners do better with immediate or delayed feedback, and how to verify answers and figures on open topics with no expert solution. For motivation, it is unknown whether interest-led paths retain as well as algorithmic queues, how spaced review should serve learners who study in bursts, which forgiving-continuity design avoids dependency, whether an AI working-alongside presence helps or pressures, how to adapt to medicated and unmedicated days, and where encouragement becomes prohibited exploitation under the AI Act. For the device, no one has tested whether decorative UI motion lowers learning or persistence in ADHD or autistic adults, whether a figure redrawn under the learner's finger behaves like scrubbing, whether short phone sessions build durable learning, or whether neurodivergent learners prefer fixed reminder times. Several popular claims are unverified and should not be quoted: FSRS review savings, "21 or 66 days" to a habit, "2–4 times more likely to procrastinate", Duolingo's leaderboard and "streak anxiety" numbers, Focusmate's productivity survey, Brilliant's "6x", OpenAI's Study Mode results, any gifted–perfectionism difference, and the origin of "persistent drive for autonomy".
